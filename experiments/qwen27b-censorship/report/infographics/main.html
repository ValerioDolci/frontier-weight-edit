From one censorship we knew, to others we didn't

Qwen3.8-27B knows Taiwan elects its president, but answers "no" when asked yes/no. We found the neurons that make it say so, then used them as a probe to look for what else the model censors — and re-checked 28 of the cases it surfaced.

WHAT IT
MEANS

An inside view, not a better search

Asking an outside model "is this topic sensitive?" finds more censorship (41 % vs 16 % on the same lists). The probe adds a small tail it misses (7 of 103 answers) — and shows where the censorship lives.

No refusal ≠ the facts

On Tiananmen the model picks the true account (P = 0.955), yet, forced to continue a Chinese sentence about June 4, it invents a military parade.

Checked, then corrected

Thresholds fixed in advance; two of our own claims withdrawn when the checks failed. One model, same-family replication on Qwen3-4B. Open code and data.

Judging: Claude Sonnet with a fixed prompt (second judge Gemma 4 31B: κ = 0.91). Yes/no battery: 40 Taiwan + 30 control questions; held-out half. Probe yields: Q4 answers; unlock test: bf16, one question per topic. Safety: StrongREJECT 200 (English; Chinese translation), Q4. Outside prior: Gemma rating of the same model-listed topics, same judge. Report, data and code: github.com/ValerioDolci/frontier-weight-edit
